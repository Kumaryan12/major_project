## Hypothesis and procedures

Compare the source class-balanced ridge64 head with ordinary equal-prior Euclidean class-centroid distance and equal-prior shared-covariance Mahalanobis distance. The main question is whether downweighting feature directions with high within-class variation improves subtype discrimination. It could also remove useful signal. This is a project-specific adaptation of distance-classification research, not the published Simple CNAPS architecture or an established MI improvement. [Research background](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.03432).

Each patient contributes one mean 64-feature vector from all retained beats of its selected recording. StandardScaler uses fitting patients only, identical to the ridge control. Centroids are arithmetic fitting-patient means in this standardized space. All six classes must be present. Let S be the average of the six class population residual covariances, each computed with divisor n_c, so classes have equal mass and patients within a class have equal mass. Use tau=max(trace(S)/64,1e-12), then C_lambda=(1-lambda)*S/tau+lambda*I. Symmetrize and apply the fixed eigenvalue floor 1e-6 before inversion. Candidates are lambda [1,.9,.5]. This trace normalization keeps the covariance scale common to the identity control; the floor protects numerical degeneracy and is not tuned.

The Euclidean head uses precision I. Both distance procedures convert negative squared distance divided by 128 (=2*64) to probabilities with softmax. This fixed mapping and equal class priors are not fitted calibration. At lambda=1, Mahalanobis must reproduce Euclidean probabilities exactly within 1e-12 absolute/relative tolerance in every context. Shared covariance determinants and priors are constant across classes. The source ridge retains its original softmax score mapping, balanced weights and inner alpha [1,100] selection; distance-versus-ridge therefore compares whole procedures, not solely covariance use.
